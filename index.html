Wetlands are cool places:

High evapotranspiration reduces surface temperature in prairie wetlands

Abstract

Wetlands can enhance biodiversity, carbon sequestration, and water quality. These systems can also provide additional climatic regulation via evapotranspiration, cooling their surrounding environment by changing heat flux partitioning, altering cooling on local and global scales. However, wetlands continue to be threatened by agriculture and development.

In this study, we established the presence of a significant cooling effect within four prairie pothole wetlands located in southern Alberta. Surface/air temperature difference within wetlands was compared to adjacent grassland areas using a thermal remote sensing workflow. We then verified our findings at Frank Lake, AB, using in-situ meteorological data and drone-based thermal imaging.
